## Understanding and engineering microbes that eat CO₂

All of us humans are heterotrophs, meaning we rely on external sources of organic matter to gain energy and matter to go on living. Plants and many diverse microbes are autotrophs, meaning they can use CO2 as their sole source of C atoms. We use the methods of environmental microbiology to study the diversity of this metabolism and learn how different autotrophic pathways affect growth.

Examples: [Flamholz et al. PNAS 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2210539119), [eLife 2020](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59882).

## Learning environmental chemistry from DNA sequences

Wherever we look, we find that wild microbes are adapted to their local habitats. What can we learn about the environment from the species and genes we find there?

Example: [Flamholz et al. mSystems 2024](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/msystems.00763-24).

## Collective CO₂ production by microbial communities

We are interested in building a quantitative understanding of collective microbial metabolism. When microbes live and eat together, as in soils or oceans, what equations should we write to predict their metabolic rates?

Example: [Goyal et al. PNAS 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2309387120).

## Fundamentals of microbial physiology and metabolism

Since we are focused on microbial metabolism in natural settings, we pursue basic questions in microbiology, systems biology, and ecology. In the example below, we developed a mathematical model that links intracellular metabolism to the extracellular environment using concepts from chemistry, finding that this model identified a novel aspect of protein evolution.

Example: [Flamholz et al. PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2404048121).

## Environmental analysis

We contribute to analyses of human choices (e.g., meat-eating) and of proposed biotechnologies (e.g., microbial bioproduction).

Examples: [Eshel et al. PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2404329122), [Lovat et al. Nat. Biotechnol. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-025-02632-w).
