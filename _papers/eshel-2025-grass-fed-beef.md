---
title: US grass-fed beef is as carbon intensive as industrial beef and ≈10-fold more intensive than common protein-dense alternatives
doi: 10.1073/pnas.2404329122
description: Grass feeding is often promoted as a lower-impact way to produce beef. Even with generous credit for soil carbon sequestration, grass-fed beef is no less carbon intensive than industrial beef.
---

The high resource intensity of industrial beef has prompted growing interest in alternative production pathways, and grass feeding is often promoted as one with a lower environmental impact. That claim needs rigorous quantification. Led by Gidon Eshel, we combined empirical data with a model built on standard equations of beef cattle performance to estimate the greenhouse gas emissions of grass-fed beef, simulating widely varied herds: from extensive operations on semiarid, marginal rangelands to partially intensive ones in lusher settings.

Per kilogram of protein, even the most efficient grass-fed beef emits 10–25% more than industrial US beef and 3- to over 40-fold more than a wide range of plant and animal alternatives. Crediting rangeland grazing with enhanced soil carbon sequestration lowers grass-fed emissions from 280–390 to 180–290 kg CO₂eq per kg protein, still at or above industrial beef (180–220) and well above non-beef alternatives (10–70). These conclusions hold across many combinations of operation type, management practice, and feed quality.
