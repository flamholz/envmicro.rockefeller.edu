When this work began, it was unclear whether we knew all the genes making up the carboxysome-based CO₂-concentrating mechanism (CCM) found in bacteria, an exquisite photosynthetic adaptation. About 15 genes were thought to be involved, but it was conceivable that the number was much higher. Our goal was to determine (i) whether unknown genes are required for the CCM and (ii) which genes perform the essential inorganic carbon transport activity.

Using a barcoded approach to TnSeq called [RB-TnSeq](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/mbio.00306-15), we investigated 70,000 knockout mutants in the bacterial chemoautotroph *H. neapolitanus*. We found that at most 17 genes are necessary for this organism's CCM to function. Building on recent work from Kathleen Scott ([2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/1462-2920.14090), [2019](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.02096-18)), we also showed that a pair of genes, DabAB2, is necessary and sufficient for inorganic carbon transport. We think the data imply that DABs are two-protein complexes that convert extracellular CO₂ into intracellular HCO₃⁻ using an energy-coupled, carbonic anhydrase-like mechanism.

More background in [this post](https://flamholz.github.io/publications/2019/08/12/DABS-are-carbon-pumps.html).
