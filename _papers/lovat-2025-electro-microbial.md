---
title: Electro-microbial production techno-economic viability and environmental implications
doi: 10.1038/s41587-025-02632-w
description: Where could microbes fed on renewable electricity and CO₂ compete with today's foods and fuels? Lower-bound calculations from basic principles point to foods, not fuels.
---

Producing goods such as foods and fuels with minimal environmental impact is urgently needed. Advances in bioproduction are promising, but it is often unclear whether, and where, new processes can compete with existing ones on economic and environmental grounds.

Led by Samuel Lovat, we made transparent, lower-bound calculations from basic principles for electro-microbial production: microbes that grow on CO₂ and energy carriers like hydrogen, formate or methanol made with renewable electricity. Energy and other costs come to roughly a dollar each per kilogram of dry biomass, which makes high-value foods and chemicals promising in the coming decade, staple foods and feed dependent on major improvements such as cheaper electricity, and bulk fuels uncompetitive even then. Compared with current products, microbial biomass shows potential environmental benefits as a food, but not as a fuel.
