The bacterial CO₂-concentrating mechanism (CCM) is a complex, multicomponent system that enables all modern cyanobacteria to grow robustly in today's atmosphere. The CCM is so tightly integrated into its host bacteria that mutants lacking any one of ≈15 genes coding for core CCM components fail to grow in today's atmosphere (≈0.04% CO₂). If every piece of the CCM is required for its modern function, how could it have evolved?

Working with several colleagues, including technician Eli Dugan, we used the tools of synthetic biology to "resurrect" ancestral forms of bacterial CCMs by engineering modern bacteria to resemble ancient ones. These experiments helped us understand how CCMs might have evolved in a piecemeal fashion, accruing individual components over billions of years as Earth's atmosphere slowly changed. Collaborating with [Woody Fischer](https://www.gps.caltech.edu/people/woodward-fischer) (Caltech Geobiology) on this research deepened our interest in Earth science.
