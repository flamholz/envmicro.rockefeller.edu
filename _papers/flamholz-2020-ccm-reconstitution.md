---
title: Functional reconstitution of a bacterial CO₂ concentrating mechanism in Escherichia coli
doi: 10.7554/eLife.59882
description: '"What I cannot create, I do not understand." We built a bacterial CO₂-concentrating mechanism from its constituent parts in *E. coli*.'
---

Many photosynthetic organisms have evolved CO₂-concentrating mechanisms (CCMs) that compensate for the relative inefficiencies of carbon fixation by elevating CO₂ levels near rubisco, the central enzyme of the Calvin cycle. Elevated CO₂ is doubly beneficial, bringing rubisco closer to CO₂ saturation and also excluding O₂ from the active site (explained [here](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.076)). CCMs are very significant on the global scale, responsible for perhaps 50% of global photosynthesis. They are also very significant to the organisms harboring them: CCM mutants typically fail entirely to grow in ambient CO₂ (0.04%) and require CO₂ supplementation (usually 1–5%) for robust growth.

In this paper we paid homage to Richard Feynman's famous quote, "[What I cannot create, I do not understand](https://calisphere.org/item/b3e8d3cb9b8adc01314dba1b1f1fcf84/)," building a bacterial CO₂-concentrating mechanism from its constituent parts in a non-native host, *E. coli*.

More background in [this post](https://flamholz.github.io/publications/2020/10/21/functional-reconstitution.html).
