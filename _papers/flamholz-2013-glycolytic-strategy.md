---
title: Glycolytic strategy as a tradeoff between energy yield and protein cost
doi: 10.1073/pnas.1215283110
description: Why do so many bacteria use lower-yield glycolytic pathways? Higher thermodynamic favorability may make them better suited to carrying high flux.
---

We offer a possible thermodynamic explanation for why so many bacteria appear to use non-canonical glycolytic pathways, especially the Entner–Doudoroff (ED) pathway. The textbook Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas (EMP) pathway yields 2 ATP per glucose, while most non-canonical pathways yield less. Naively we'd expect cells to switch to higher-yielding pathways, but they appear not to. Lower-yield pathways are more thermodynamically favorable, which, we suggest, makes them better suited to carrying high flux. This was a close collaboration with [Elad Noor](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=E4UymDMAAAAJ&hl=en), with major contributions from Arren Bar-Even, Wolfram Liebermeister, and Ron Milo.

Arion Stettner and Daniel Segrè wrote a nice [commentary](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1307485110) on this work. [Fuhrer et al. (*J. Bacteriol.* 2005)](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/jb.187.5.1581-1590.2005) from the [Sauer lab](https://imsb.ethz.ch/research/sauer.html) at ETH was a major motivation for this study. Since then, elegant experiments have shown that the ED pathway is likely present in plants and cyanobacteria ([Chen et al., *PNAS* 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1521916113)) and is a major route of bacterial degradation of sulfonated sugars derived from plant membranes ([Felux et al., *PNAS* 2015](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1507049112)).
