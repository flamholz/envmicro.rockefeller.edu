---
title: A map of the rubisco biochemical landscape
doi: 10.1038/s41586-024-08455-0
description: A massively parallel assay in rubisco-dependent E. coli measured nearly every single-amino-acid mutant of rubisco, mapping how sequence shapes its speed and CO₂ affinity.
---

Rubisco is the primary CO₂-fixing enzyme of the biosphere, yet its kinetics are slow, and the roles of evolution and chemical mechanism in constraining its function remain debated. Efforts to engineer better rubiscos have largely failed, though recent results suggest its functional potential is broader than previously appreciated.

Led by Noam Prywes, this work developed a massively parallel assay that couples rubisco activity to the growth of an engineered *E. coli* strain, and used it to systematically map the sequence–function landscape of the enzyme. Assaying more than 99% of single-amino-acid mutants across CO₂ concentrations allowed us to infer velocity and apparent CO₂ affinity for thousands of substitutions. The map reveals many highly conserved positions that nonetheless tolerate mutation, as well as rare mutations that improve CO₂ affinity. Non-trivial biochemical changes are readily accessible, and the functional distance between rubiscos from diverse organisms can be traversed, laying groundwork for future engineering.
