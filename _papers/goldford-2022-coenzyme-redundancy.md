---
title: Protein cost minimization promotes the emergence of coenzyme redundancy
doi: 10.1073/pnas.2110787119
description: Why does metabolism use structurally redundant coenzymes like NAD(H) and NADP(H)? Redundancy may be an evolved strategy for using enzymes efficiently.
---

Metabolism relies on a small set of coenzymes that serve as universal donors and acceptors of key chemical groups and electrons. Metabolic networks depend on structurally redundant coenzymes, such as NAD(H) and NADP(H), that are used by different enzymes, but why this redundancy arose is poorly understood.

Combining metabolic modeling with structural and sequence analysis, we find that coenzyme redundancy may not be essential for metabolism. Rather, it could be an evolved strategy for using enzymes efficiently when biochemical reactions operate near equilibrium. This suggests that early metabolism may have run on fewer coenzymes, and that selection for metabolic efficiency drove the diversification of coenzymes in living systems. Led by Joshua Goldford, with Ashish George and Daniel Segrè.
