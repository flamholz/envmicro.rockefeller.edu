---
title: Optical O₂ Sensors Also Respond to Redox Active Molecules Commonly Secreted by Bacteria
doi: 10.1128/mbio.02076-22
description: Many bacteria secrete molecules that interfere with optical O₂ sensors. We document the problem and offer two ways around it.
---

Photosynthesis makes organic molecules from CO₂ and water by harnessing the energy of light; respiration, the other arm of the biological carbon cycle, uses the energy of O₂ (its tendency to take electrons) to break those same molecules down, piece by piece, forming CO₂ again. We set out to measure the respiratory rates of single bacterial colonies to understand how "microenvironments" are formed and maintained.

In doing so, we encountered a challenge that was not documented in the literature: many bacteria secrete molecules that greatly interfere with optical O₂ sensors. This paper documents the problem and offers two avenues to solve it. First, some bacteria, e.g., *E. coli*, secrete no problematic molecules. Second, it matters what the sensors are made of: materials engineering might protect optical nanosensors from interference by bacterial secretions.
